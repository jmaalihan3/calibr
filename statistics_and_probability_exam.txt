UNIT 12: SIGNIFICANCE TESTS (HYPOTHESIS TESTING)
======================================================================

Question 16
In hypothesis testing, what does the null hypothesis (H0) typically represent?
  A) The claim that the researcher hopes to prove true.
  B) A statement of "no difference," "no effect," or status quo.
  C) Always a directional (one-tailed) inequality statement.
  D) The probability of making a Type II error.

Question 17
What is a Type I error in significance testing?
  A) Rejecting a null hypothesis when it is actually true.
  B) Failing to reject a null hypothesis when it is actually true.
  C) Rejecting a null hypothesis when it is actually false.
  D) Failing to reject a null hypothesis when a better alternative exists.

Question 18
If a calculated P-value for a two-tailed test is 0.03 and the chosen significance level is alpha = 0.05, what is the appropriate conclusion?
  A) Fail to reject H0; there is sufficient evidence of an effect.
  B) Reject H0; there is statistically significant evidence against the null hypothesis.
  C) Accept H0 as absolute truth.
  D) Redo the experiment because the P-value is too close to alpha.

Question 19
What does the P-value measure in a hypothesis test?
  A) The probability that the alternative hypothesis is true.
  B) The probability that the null hypothesis is true.
  C) The probability of observing results as extreme as (or more extreme than) those observed, assuming the null hypothesis is true.
  D) The fixed risk of committing a Type I error set by the researcher prior to the test.

Question 20
When should a researcher choose a one-tailed (one-sided) significance test instead of a two-tailed test?
  A) When sample sizes are smaller than 30.
  B) When previous research or theory strongly suggests the population parameter only increases or only decreases relative to the null value.
  C) Whenever they want to reduce the probability of a Type I error to zero.
  D) When the data distribution is heavily skewed.
